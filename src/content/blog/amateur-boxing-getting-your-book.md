Every so often somebody finishes a class, hangs back, and asks the question a
little sideways: *what would it take to actually fight?*

Here's the real path.

## First, you train

There is no shortcut past this and we won't pretend otherwise. Before anyone
steps into the Fighters Program, they put in months in the regular classes: fundamentals, mitt work, conditioning, and eventually supervised sparring.

You don't ask to move up. A coach tells you when you're ready. That's not
gatekeeping for its own sake; it's the difference between a first fight that
builds someone and a first fight that ends their interest in the sport.

## What the book is

USA Boxing is the national governing body for amateur boxing. To compete in a
sanctioned amateur bout you need to be a registered member, pass a physical,
and carry a **passbook**: the record of your registration, your medicals, and
every bout you've had.

No book, no fight. It's the sport's safety system, and it's non-negotiable.

## How you get one

Register with USA Boxing, complete the medical requirements, and get your
passbook issued through the association. It sounds bureaucratic written down;
in practice a coach walks you through the whole thing. Nobody at Ironhand has
had to figure this out alone.

## The Fighters Program

Once you're registered, you train **Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday at 7:00 PM**
with the competition group. It's $150 per month, and you need your book to be
in it.

The work is different from a group class. More rounds. More ring time. More
attention on the specific things that will get exposed against a live opponent, defense, distance, output, and what you do when you're tired and behind.

## Who this is for

Not everybody, and that's the point. Most Ironhand members never compete and
get everything they came for anyway.

But if you've been in the gym a while and the question keeps coming back to
you, come ask it out loud. We'll tell you honestly where you are and what the
next six months would need to look like.
